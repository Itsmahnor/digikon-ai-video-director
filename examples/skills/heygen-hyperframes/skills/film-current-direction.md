**When to use:** at the start of motion design for any multi-scene film.

**Rule:** choose one dominant direction (the "current", left by default) and use it for every ordinary seam, so the film reads as one continuous camera move. Treat other vectors as reserved and meaningful: upward means elevation (a conclusion rises above what came before); a push through depth means going deeper into the same thought; a pull back means arrival (something bigger lands); a scale burst means leaving a world.

**Example:** a SaaS film moves left between feature scenes, rises for the result slide, and pulls back for the logo arrival.

**Caution:** spending a reserved vector on a routine cut cheapens it.

**Check:** list the direction of every seam in order; any departure from the film's main direction should have a stated reason.
