**When to use:** designing seams and in-scene action.

**Carriers:** the eye follows objects, not abstractions. The strongest seams pass a concrete carrier across the cut at matched position and velocity: a cursor mid-path, a container that shrinks into the next layout, a mark flying into its slot, the word group of a waterfall cut. Without a natural carrier, the scene's hero elements carry it, with partial travel and an early fade. Never use a plain crossfade, which carries nothing.

**Causal motion:** chain each move so it is visibly launched by the last (click, squash, release, flight, impact, recoil, reveal). Effects start on the causing frame, not shortly after. Reactions scale with implied mass: big elements rebound slowly, small ones snap. A direction change needs a visible cause or a chapter boundary.

**Example:** the cursor clicks "Send"; on the same frame the button squashes and the card flies up into the next scene's header slot.
