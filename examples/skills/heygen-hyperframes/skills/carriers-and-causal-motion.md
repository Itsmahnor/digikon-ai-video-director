**When to use:** designing seams and in-scene action.

**Carriers:** the eye follows objects, not abstractions. The strongest seams hand the viewer something tangible that keeps travelling through the cut, at the same place and speed on both sides: for example a pointer part-way along its path, a panel that shrinks and docks into the next layout, a logo flying to its position, or the words of a text-to-text cut. Without a natural carrier, the scene's hero elements carry it, with partial travel and an early fade. Never use a plain crossfade, which carries nothing.

**Causal motion:** chain each move so it is visibly launched by the last (click, squash, release, flight, impact, recoil, reveal). Effects start on the causing frame, not shortly after. Reactions scale with implied mass: big elements rebound slowly, small ones snap. A direction change needs a visible cause or a chapter boundary.

**Example:** the cursor clicks "Send"; on the same frame the button squashes and the card flies up into the next scene's header slot.

**Rule:** if a seam has no carrier, change the plan until one exists; do not mask the gap with a crossfade.
